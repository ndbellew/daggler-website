## Summary

This course project proposes upgrading the access-control platform of the hypothetical company Really Cool Locks LLC from a single-core card-reader-and-lock system to a multicore operating environment. It outlines hardware, firmware, and software migration steps and combines preemptive priority scheduling with multilevel-feedback queues to separate emergency operations from routine workloads.

The proposed four-core design assigns responsibilities for emergency response, normal inputs, authentication and sensor processing, and logging and networking. It considers event-driven and shared-memory concurrency, then identifies risks including priority inversion, shared-memory corruption, privilege escalation, event flooding, insecure updates, and log protection. The document also considers micro-VMs, edge computing, and trusted execution environments as future enhancements.

The paper presents a proposed architecture and mitigation strategy. It does not report an implemented system or measured performance results.
